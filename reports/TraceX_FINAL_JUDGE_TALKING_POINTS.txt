2. KEY DESIGN DECISIONS (Defend the architecture)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Why DuckDB? "We chose DuckDB because it operates purely in-memory and columnar. Processing a 4-hop BFS graph over 2M edges using standard SQLite or Pandas would take minutes. TraceX does it in 6.5 seconds. It scales without heavy infrastructure."
- Why React-Force-Graph? "Mule networks are naturally massive graphs (often 20,000+ nodes). We used WebGL acceleration on the canvas to ensure the browser doesn't freeze during panning, zooming, or timeline scrubbing."
- The AI Anti-Hallucination Guardrail: "Generative AI often invents facts when faced with too much data. We built an 'Evidence-First' AI layer. It doesn't query the database—it only receives the 50 exact transactions the investigator manually isolates, guaranteeing that every PDF freeze requisition is 100% legally accurate."

3. TERMINOLOGY DISCIPLINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Say: "Risk Indicator" (Not "Fraudster")
- Say: "Anomaly detected" (Not "Criminal")
- Say: "Money Mule Network" or "Layering pattern" (Not "Guilty account")
- Say: "Evidence Subdataset"
- Why? "TraceX respects the legal presumption of innocence. It is a decision-support tool, not a judge."

4. HANDLING THE "WHAT ABOUT L2 DISTRIBUTORS?" QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If asked: "Your graph truncates at 2000 nodes per layer, aren't you losing data?"
Answer: "Yes, by design. In a real-world scenario, capturing a Level 2 distributor who fans out to 15,000 retail accounts creates noise, not actionable intelligence for a single cyber cell. TraceX bounds layer sizes during BFS to focus on the strongest, most immediate mule links while keeping the UI hyper-responsive. An investigator can click on any border node to re-center the graph and traverse further."
