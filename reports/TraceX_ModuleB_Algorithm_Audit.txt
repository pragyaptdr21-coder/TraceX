12. JUDGE ANSWERS
Q1. What algorithm do you use for high-velocity detection?
A: A windowed temporal self-join in DuckDB that pairs an incoming transaction to all subsequent outgoing transactions from that same account within 3-15 minutes.

Q2. How do you calculate the 90% threshold?
A: We sum the amounts of the outgoing transactions occurring in the 3-15 minute window, and trigger the flag if that sum divided by the specific incoming transaction amount is >= 0.90.

Q3. How do you detect Collector Mules?
A: By executing a fan-in centrality graph query, calculating the `COUNT(DISTINCT Sender_Account)` per receiver, and applying an engineering threshold of >= 5 distinct senders to filter out standard ecommerce noise.

Q4. How do you detect Distributor Mules?
A: By executing a fan-out centrality graph query, grouping outgoing transactions by sender, and enforcing the exact PDF constraint that the distinct downstream receivers fall strictly between 3 and 7.

Q5. What graph features are used?
A: In-degree (distinct senders), Out-degree (distinct receivers), sequential loops (3-hop cycles), and Breadth-First traversal maps.

Q6. How do you detect terminal cash-out?
A: By substring-matching narrations for wallet/crypto/p2p text, prefix-matching IPs for foreign blocks (185.x, 194.x), and exact-matching device headers for emulator scripts.

Q7. What algorithm do you use for multi-hop tracing?
A: A layer-based Breadth-First Search (BFS) graph traversal.

Q8. Why BFS?
A: BFS guarantees finding the shortest paths iteratively and allows us to easily enforce a firm hop-limit (depth boundary) while systematically building the layer array required by the frontend visualization.

Q9. How many hops?
A: Exactly 4 hops downstream.

Q10. What is the measured 4-hop latency?
A: Evaluated across 5 random arbitrary accounts spanning the 2M row graph, it averages ~1.48 seconds per trace.

Q11. Can judges give an arbitrary victim account?
A: Yes. The traversal queries the indexed graph dynamically in real-time based on any arbitrary string input.

Q12. How is the detection result converted into a Mule Risk Index?
A: Extracted detection arrays are fed into the Risk Engine which assigns localized scores (e.g., +25 for Velocity, +50 for Terminal Indicators) up to a rigid deterministic cap of 100. Each point addition is linked to the evidence object of the triggering algorithm.
